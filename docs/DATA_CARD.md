### The label definition — the one thing to get right

`label = 1` means the comment **identifies a concrete action the agency could be
asked to take, or that the commenter proposes the agency take**. It does *not*
mean the comment contains a politeness marker.

> *Please*, *dapat*, *sana*, *paki-* are **evidence**, not the definition. A comment
> with *please* that only asks the reader to vote is `other`; a comment with no
> marker at all that says the road must be fixed is `actionable`.

The paper's research question asks whether adding these markers as features
improves detection over a plain TF-IDF baseline. A marker-based labeling scheme
would make the answer trivially yes and the study would measure nothing, so the
labels were not defined that way.

**Always use `pos_label=1`.** A flipped `pos_label` inverts every metric.

```python
classification_report(y, pred, pos_label=1, zero_division=0)
f1_score(y, pred, pos_label=1)
confusion_matrix(y, pred, labels=[0, 1])   # [[TN, FP], [FN, TP]]
```

**`comment_text_norm` and nothing else.** Never concatenate `post_text` or

## 4. Known limitations — read this before drawing conclusions

**The corpus is concentrated.** A small number of posts carry a large share of
the comments, because a post on a high-traffic account draws far more comments
than the rest.

### The largest posts

| Post | Comments | actionable | actionable % |
|---|---|---|---|
| d_c7adea76301e5bdc | 209 | 9 | 4.3% |
| h_24364576a353a509 | 204 | 49 | 24.0% |
| h_c6df40e102162fdf | 106 | 18 | 17.0% |
| h_77fb0b7cb1f05e9e | 85 | 9 | 10.6% |
| g_55360bfda32fa270 | 85 | 14 | 16.5% |
| h_8da3cbe8f169597b | 32 | 7 | 21.9% |
| h_80b98a13f1a42f4f | 30 | 7 | 23.3% |
| h_a5eff28a3c3e499b | 29 | 4 | 13.8% |
| h_3f0026fd16fae3dc | 23 | 2 | 8.7% |
| d_2d702ca032c46ec7 | 21 | 10 | 47.6% |

The ten largest posts hold **824 of 1,657 rows (49.7%)**.
A random-split classifier can therefore score well by recognising *which post a
comment came from* rather than what it says. The split is grouped by `post_id`
precisely so that a post's comments never straddle two splits — otherwise the
model is scored on its own vocabulary.

**Reddit contributes nothing.** No Philippine national government agency runs a
subreddit. The one candidate that is about government, `r/PHGov`, is a community
and fails the eligibility criteria S1 and S2. The reasoning is in
`data/provenance/PLATFORM_COVERAGE.md`.

**The request-marker rate is not the actionable rate.** It is an upper bound
computed from surface markers, used only to describe the corpus. The label comes
from the annotator.

**The post caption is missing on some rows.** `post_text` is the post context -
the caption of the post the comment sits on. It is empty for 553 of 1,657 rows (33%), because the extractor that
reads the caption was corrected after those captures were made. No caption is
**wrong**: the ones that are present are real captions. This does not touch the
modelling text field, which is `comment_text_norm` alone, and `post_text` is
context for annotation and for reporting, not a feature.

**The extension platforms are thin.** YouTube contributes 101 comments and X fewer. The YouTube application stopped
rendering in the driven tabs partway through the collection, so its comments
could not be read from the page; the videos themselves were found from the
page's own data. The primary dataset is Facebook only, which is what the
study's scope describes, and the extension is there for a robustness check
rather than as the study's dataset.

**The per-source post count is a floor, not the planned eight to twelve.**
The collection plan takes the most recent posts of each source. Under
continuous collection Facebook serves only the first one or two posts of
a page's feed and does not serve more on scrolling or on a reload, so 37 of 44 sources
contribute fewer than eight posts. The pages themselves load - the follower
count, the category and the description all render - and a tab created for
the page serves the whole feed after the session has rested, which is what
identifies this as a rate limit rather than a broken collector or an
inactive account. The shortfall
is recorded per account rather than smoothed away, and the rule that
forbids choosing posts by comment richness was kept, because the
alternative - walking backwards until a post is busy - is exactly the
cherry-picking that rule exists to prevent.

**Only five of the agencies clear the contract's forty-comment floor.**
Contract 11 asks for at least forty usable comments per source page, so
that no page dominates and no page is a statistical singleton.
5 of 44 agencies clear it:

| agency | rows |
|---|---|
| `dpwh` | 502 |
| `deped` | 268 |
| `dilg` | 151 |
| `doh` | 77 |
| `OfficeOfTheVicePresidentPH` | 47 |

The other 39 sit between 2 and 39 rows, and the three largest
agencies hold 56% of the corpus. This matters for how the
results can be read: the model learns most of its vocabulary from one or two
agencies and is then tested on thirty others, so a good score is not by
itself evidence that the method generalises across government. Treat the
per-agency numbers as the honest unit of analysis, not the pooled F1.

The column was empty in every row until 2026-10-23, so this concentration
went unseen while the dataset was being built; see the open register, §3.9.

**A couple of rows are Cebuano or Boholano rather than Filipino.** The
exclusion rule for a non-Filipino language is a script check, and those
languages are written in Latin script, so they pass it. They are labelled
`other`, which is right in every case where they were a reaction, and they
are counted here rather than quietly filtered.

## 5. How the labels were produced

**LLM-assisted annotation with model-assisted verification.** This is not manual
annotation by a human researcher. The verification pass was performed by an
independent model annotator blind to the first pass, not by a human. The
dataset records `verification = llm_verified`, and the metadata records
`verification_method = 'llm_assisted'`. The write-up
must say **model-assisted verification**, never human verification.

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1. Cue extraction | The engine extracts request markers, action verbs, strong request frames, action objects, complaint markers, gratitude, reaction, information questions and refusals from each comment. |
| 2. Heuristic scoring | Each comment gets a suggested label, a confidence and the rule that fired. |
| 3. Adjudication | Every row below 0.85 confidence was adjudicated against `docs/ANNOTATION_GUIDELINES.md` by an LLM working from the written decision tree, plus a random 10% audit sample of the rows the engine could decide. |
| 4. Verification | 120 rows re-read blind; agreement 93.3%; Cohen's κ = 0.79. 136 rows carry `verification = llm_verified` after adjudication. |
| 5. Blinded re-label | 332 rows re-labelled blind; κ = 0.72. Washout: 0 days. |

Twenty-four pass-1 labels were corrected during adjudication against the blind
pass. The corrections are listed in `data/provenance/adjudication_log.csv` and
are not included in the κ, which measures the pre-adjudication agreement.

The disputed class — a request marker with an object but no action verb, for
example *"Sana maayos ang kalsada"* — is never auto-decided. It always routes
to adjudication, because it is where the guidelines disagree most.

**The reliability statistic is intra-annotator reliability (test-retest), not
inter-annotator agreement.** There is one annotator. The re-label ran without a
washout, so κ is an upper bound rather than a true test-retest estimate. A
human spot-check is still recommended before the paper claims human verification.

## 8. Prohibitions

- Do not use `post_text` or `context_text_norm` in the primary feature space.
- Do not use a `pos_label` other than 1.
- Do not re-split the data. The splits are in the package, grouped by `post_id`.
- Do not re-number or re-use a `comment_id`.
- Do not treat the request-marker rate as the actionable rate.
